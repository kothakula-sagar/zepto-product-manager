Start with a core product skill

Every product decision starts with a user problem, not a feature idea.

FIRST PRINCIPLE

Separate what you observed from what you assume

A rider may report “I waited too long.” That is a symptom. The cause could be order readiness, assignment timing, store congestion, a wrong status, navigation, or something else. Ask what evidence would distinguish those causes before proposing a fix.

YOUR FIRST PROJECT

Investigate rider pickup-to-delivery friction

Map the journey, identify where time or uncertainty is created, propose a small product change, and define rider, customer, and business guardrails.

Important: This is an independent learning project based on a hypothetical scenario. Do not imply you worked at Zepto or had access to its rider app, private data, or internal processes.

Project brief

A hypothetical quick-commerce operation hears that some delivery partners wait at pickup points. Your task is to investigate possible causes and recommend a small, testable improvement. Do not assume that any particular assignment, picker, or queue rule exists; treat each as a hypothesis to validate.

Portfolio title suggestion: “Reducing uncertainty in a quick-commerce rider pickup journey — independent product case study.”
